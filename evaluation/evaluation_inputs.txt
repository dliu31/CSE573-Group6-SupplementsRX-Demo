# Calcium
Input: For calcium, how much should my daily intake be?

# Zinc
Input: For zinc, how much should my daily intake be?
Expected_Output: The recommended daily intake for zinc ranges from 2-8 milligrams for children, and 11 milligrams for teens and older. Please consult with a medical professional for your needs.
Input: What are food sources for it?
Expected_Output: The richest food sources of zinc include meat, fish, and seafood. Eggs and dairy products also contain zinc. Beans, nuts, and whole grains contain zinc. Zinc fortified foods can also be a good source.
Input: What are the risks of taking too much of it?
Expected_Output: High zinc intakes can cause nausea, dizziness, headaches, gastric distress, vomiting, and loss of appetite. Excessive intake can also reduce copper absorption (which can cause low copper status), reduce immune function, and lower HDL cholesterol levels.
Input: What are most of the risks of having a deficiency in it?
Expected_Output: In infants and children, diarrhea is a common sign. In older children, alopecia, delayed growth, and frequent infections become more prevalent. In both infants and children, zinc deficiency can impair growth and lead to a loss of appetite and reproductive problems when they reach adulthood. In populations with low intakes of absorbable zinc (e.g., from meat and fish), zinc deficiency affects the health of pregnant women and their infants by increasing the risk of child morbidity (including premature birth and low birthweight) and mortality, maternal morbidity, and adverse birth outcomes. In addition, zinc deficiency can interfere with the senses of taste and smell. Zinc deficiency in older adults can cause delays in wound healing and changes in cognitive and psychological function.
# Selenium
Input: What is the risk of selenium deficiency?
Expected_Output: On its own, selenium deficiency rarely causes illness. However, in certain populations, selenium deficiency may trigger the development of conditions such as Keshan disease and Kashin-Beck disease. In addition, selenium deficiency may increase the risk of congenital hypothyroidism in infants by exacerbating iodine deficiency.
Input: What are sources for selenium?
Expected_Output: Foods that are high in protein, such as seafood, meat, and poultry, are rich sources of selenium. Brazil nuts contain particularly high amounts of selenium. The selenium concentrations of plant-based foods vary by geographic location.
Input: What is the risk of taking too much selenium?
Expected_Output: Too much selenium can cause selenosis.
Input: What is selenosis and what harmful effects does it cause on the body?
Expected_Output: Selenosis is most commonly characterized by hair loss and nail brittleness or loss, but other signs and symptoms can include a garlic odor in the breath, a metallic taste in the mouth, skin rash, nausea, diarrhea, fatigue, irritability, and nervous system abnormalities.
